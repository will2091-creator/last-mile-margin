Day rate, per stop, or hourly?

  • Day rate is simple and predictable for the crew, but you carry the cost on light days.
  • Per stop moves with volume. A light day costs you less, but your crew's pay swings, which can make hiring harder.
  • Hourly pays for the time, so long days with few stops cost you more.

The "When stop counts swing" table shows all of this with your numbers: the same hours with 25% fewer or more stops.

What goes into payroll costs

On top of wages, employers pay Social Security and Medicare (7.65%), federal and state unemployment tax, and workers' comp. Workers' comp for delivery crews varies a lot by state and can be a big line. The calculator starts at 15%; swap in the real rate from your payroll provider.

Questions

Do I owe overtime on a day rate or per-stop pay?

Under federal law, most non-exempt employees are owed overtime for hours over 40 in a week, including those paid by the day or by the stop. For them, overtime adds half the regular rate for each extra hour. Some drivers are exempt under the Motor Carrier Act, depending on truck weight and the type of hauling, and state rules can be stricter. Check with your payroll provider or an employment attorney.

What's a healthy labor share?

The calculator flags crew cost above 35% of route revenue. Past that, it's hard to cover the truck, fuel, insurance, and claims and still make money.
