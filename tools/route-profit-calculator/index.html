Guide

How the calculator works

It runs the same math as the Route Profit Check in Last Mile Margin:

  • Revenue is your flat route pay, plus pay per stop times stops, plus installs, accessorials, fuel surcharge, and re-attempt pay.
  • Fuel is miles divided by miles per gallon, times the fuel price. Maintenance is miles times your cost per mile.
  • Costs add crew pay, truck payment, insurance, phones and software, tolls and parking, claims, and anything else.
  • Margin is net profit divided by revenue.

The grade weighs daily profit, margin, profit per stop, and profit per mile. A route that loses money is an F no matter how busy it is. Below about 15% margin, the calculator warns you, because one damage claim or one repair can wipe out the day.

Costs owners forget

  • Miles to and from the warehouse, not just between stops.
  • Insurance and the truck payment on the days the truck runs, not per calendar day.
  • Claims and chargebacks averaged across the days you ran, even the clean ones.
  • Scanner, phone, and software fees.
  • Re-attempts you don't get paid for.

FAQ

Questions contractors ask

How do I figure my daily truck and insurance cost?

Divide the monthly bill by the days the truck actually runs. A $2,000 truck payment over 22 workdays is about $91 a day.

Should I count my own pay?

If you drive or ride on the truck, put your pay in as driver or helper pay. Otherwise the profit number is really your wages.

My contract pays per stop. What do I enter?

Set flat route pay to 0 and fill in pay per stop and stops. If you get a base plus per-stop pay, fill in both.

How should I count claims?

Add up the last three months of claim and chargeback deductions and divide by the days you ran in that time.

Is anything I enter saved or sent anywhere?

The calculator runs in your browser and remembers your numbers on this device. If you copy a share link, anyone who opens it can see the numbers it contains.
