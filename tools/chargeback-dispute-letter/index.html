How to dispute a delivery chargeback

  1. Check your dispute window. Your contract sets how long you have. Send it as soon as you can.
  2. Pull your proof. Signed proof of delivery, photos, scan and GPS records, the call-ahead log, and a statement from the crew.
  3. Ask for theirs. Damage photos, an itemized invoice, and the customer's original complaint.
  4. Send it in writing and keep a copy.
  5. Follow up and track the result, so you learn which arguments work.

Arguments that carry weight

  • A signed proof of delivery with no damage noted.
  • Damage reported days after the delivery.
  • Damage noted at pickup, before the item was in your care.
  • No photos or invoice from the other side.
  • A penalty your own scan times or call log contradict.

The letter only mentions proof you check. It never admits fault, and it always asks for the deduction to be reversed or backed up.

Questions

Is this legal advice?

No. It's a business letter template. For large or disputed amounts, talk to an attorney.

Should I dispute every chargeback?

Not always. Focus on the ones you have proof for and the bigger amounts. Track which ones you win so you know where your time pays off.

What if my crew was partly at fault?

Choose “Partly our fault.” The letter won't admit fault. It asks for proof of the actual cost and asks them to limit the deduction to it.

Is my information saved or sent anywhere?

No. The letter is built in your browser. Your company and contact details are remembered on this device; claim details aren't.
